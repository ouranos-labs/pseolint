The Great Wall of China is a series of fortifications that were built across the historical northern borders of ancient Chinese states and Imperial China as protection against various nomadic groups from the Eurasian Steppe. Several walls were built from as early as the 7th century BC, with selective stretches later joined by Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of China, in around 221 BC. Later on, many successive dynasties built and maintained multiple stretches of border walls.

The most well-known sections of the wall were built by the Ming dynasty between 1369 and 1644. The main purpose of the Great Wall was not to keep out people, who could scale the wall, but to insure that semi-nomadic people could not cross the border with their horses or return easily with stolen property. The wall also served as transportation and communication routes along the border. It was also used to control and tax trade.

Estimates of its length have varied widely over time. A 2012 archaeological survey found that the walls built by the Ming dynasty measure 8,850 km, while an earlier 2009 study had concluded that the walls built throughout China's history measure 21,196 km.

The Great Wall has become a symbol of Chinese culture and history. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987. The Great Wall of China is also one of the most popular tourist attractions in the world, attracting millions of visitors each year.

The Great Wall was built using a variety of materials, including earth, wood, stone, brick, and tile. The construction of the wall was an enormous undertaking that required vast amounts of labor and resources. The workers who built the wall were primarily soldiers, peasants, and prisoners. Many workers died during the construction of the wall, and it is estimated that millions of people were involved in its construction over the centuries.

The wall runs from the Jiayuguan Pass in the west to the Shanhaiguan Pass in the east. The western end of the wall is in the Gobi Desert, while the eastern end is near the Yellow Sea. The wall passes through a variety of terrain, including mountains, grasslands, and deserts.

The Great Wall of China has been featured in countless works of literature, art, and cinema. It has become one of the most recognizable symbols of China and is often used to represent Chinese culture and history. The wall is also the subject of many myths and legends, including the popular but false claim that it is visible from space with the naked eye.
