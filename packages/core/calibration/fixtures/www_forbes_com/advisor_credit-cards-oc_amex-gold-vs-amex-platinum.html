Few cards come with as many perks and benefits as The Platinum Card® from American Express (Terms apply, see rates & fees) and the American Express® Gold Card (Terms apply, see rates & fees). Both offer strong earning potential, solid welcome bonuses and a bevy of perks that, for the right person, can easily outweigh their annual cost of ownership.

Plus, both cards earn Membership Rewards points, a highly flexible rewards currency prized among points and miles enthusiasts for their 1:1 transfers ratio to over 15 travel partners.

That’s all well and good, but how do you decide which one is right for you?

First, despite the product names that might lead you to believe that The Platinum Card® from American Express is just the upgraded version of the American Express® Gold Card, these are very different products targeted toward consumers with different spending patterns and needs.

The American Express® Gold Card has few travel benefits but earns 4 Membership Rewards® points per dollar at restaurants (on up to $50,000 in purchases per calendar year, then 1 point per dollar beginning on 1/1/2025), 4 Membership Rewards® points per dollar at US supermarkets (on up to $25,000 in purchases per calendar year, then 1 point per dollar), 3 Membership Rewards® points per dollar on flights booked directly with airlines or on American Express Travel, 2 Membership Rewards® points per dollar on prepaid hotels and other eligible purchases booked on American Express Travel and 1 point per dollar on all other eligible purchases. If you hold the Gold Card, you’re likely to maximize its value by using it as your primary card for groceries and dining.

However, The Platinum Card® from American Express is meant for frequent travelers who want an elevated experience. The card includes airport lounge access, hotel status with two brands and lifestyle credits with cash rebates on purchases, from digital subscriptions to Uber. The Platinum Card® from American Express earns 5 Membership Rewards per dollar on select travel spending but is otherwise light on earnings in bonus categories.

Welcome Bonus

When it comes to the welcome bonus between the Gold vs. Platinum Amex cards, there isn’t much difference. The Platinum Card® from American Express and American Express® Gold Card both regularly offer generous welcome bonuses to new card members. While The Platinum Card’s welcome bonus is often more generous, it’s important to note the amount you need to spend to earn the bonus. Before signing up for a card, be sure you have a plan to spend at least that much on your card within the required timeframe.

For The Platinum Card® from American Express, earn 80,000 Membership Rewards Points after spending $8,000 on eligible purchases on the card in the first 6 months of card membership.

With the American Express® Gold Card, you can earn 60,000 Membership Rewards points after spending $6,000 on eligible purchases in the first 6 months of card membership, plus 20% back in statement credits on eligible purchases made at restaurants worldwide within the first 6 months of card membership, up to $100 back.

Winner: The Platinum Card® from American Express

Annual Fee

The Amex Platinum carries a hefty annual fee of $695, which is far higher than the Amex Gold’s recently increased annual fee of $325.

It should also be noted that the Amex Platinum charges $195 for each authorized user. Amex Gold members, meanwhile, can add up to five authorized users for free (after that, the annual fee for additional members is $35).

Winner: The American Express® Gold Card

Earning Rewards

The Platinum Card’s most generous earnings are for flights and hotels booked with American Express Travel. The card earns 5 Membership Rewards Points per dollar for flights booked directly with airlines or with American Express Travel on up to $500,000 per calendar year, 5 points per dollar on prepaid hotels booked with American Express Travel and 1 point per dollar on other eligible purchases.

If you spend more at the grocery store or restaurants, the American Express® Gold Card is rewarding as it offers big rewards in both categories. With the Gold Card, you’ll earn 4 Membership Rewards® points per dollar at restaurants (on up to $50,000 in purchases per calendar year, then 1 point per dollar beginning on 1/1/2025), 4 Membership Rewards® points per dollar at US supermarkets (on up to $25,000 in purchases per calendar year, then 1 point per dollar), 3 Membership Rewards® points per dollar on flights booked directly with airlines or on American Express Travel, 2 Membership Rewards® points per dollar on prepaid hotels and other eligible purchases booked on American Express Travel and 1 point per dollar on all other eligible purchases.

Winner: The American Express® Gold Card will be more lucrative for most people.

Travel Benefits

Travelers interested in elevating their experience at the airport should take full advantage of the lounge access privileges granted by The American Express Platinum Card. No other card will give you access to as many premium airport lounges as The Platinum Card® from American Express. Owning the card will grant you entry to the exclusive Centurion Lounge network. These lounges are reserved just for cardholders of the Platinum, The Business Platinum Card® from American Express (Terms apply, see rates & fees) and the even more exclusive invitation-only Centurion® Card from American Express*.

With The Platinum Card® from American Express, you’ll also get access to Delta Sky Club lounges when flying same-day on Delta flights, Priority Pass Select lounges and more with enrollment. If you want your airport hang time to be more comfortable and there’s an airport lounge of nearly any brand, this card has you covered.

Beginning February 1, 2025, Platinum cardholders will no longer have unlimited visits to Delta Sky Clubs. Instead, the limit will be 10 visits per year. You can earn unlimited visits by spending at least $75,000 before December 31, 2024.

Airport lounge access isn’t the end of the Platinum Card’s travel benefits. Platinum Card ownership comes with more extras than you can pack in your suitcase. You’ll also get:

  • Up to $200 in airline incidental fee statement credits on a qualifying airline you choose each year (enrollment required)
  • Statement credits of up to $189 per calendar year for CLEAR® Plus membership
  • A statement credit of up to $85 for TSA PreCheck or up to $100 for Global Entry
  • Money-saving perks and benefits at hotels booked in The Hotel Collection with American Express Travel or the Fine Hotels + Resorts Collection
  • Elite status with both Marriott Bonvoy and Hilton Honors hotel programs (after enrollment)

The Platinum Card® from American Express also comes with a raft of included protections and insurances that could give you peace of mind when you’re on the go. One protection The Platinum Card® from American Express has that the American Express® Gold Card doesn’t is trip cancellation and interruption insurance¹ for eligible claims. If your trip is canceled or interrupted for a covered reason, you can receive up to $10,000 per trip and up to $20,000 per eligible card per consecutive 12-month period. The Platinum Card® from American Express also comes with travel evacuation support². This can give you peace of mind if you miss a connecting flight or get injured, among other reasons.

However, remember that The Platinum Card® from American Express isn’t the best in class for its included travel insurance. The Amex Platinum has trip interruption and cancellation coverage, but if you want benefits like lost luggage reimbursement and access to emergency evacuation and transportation services, the Chase Sapphire Reserve® offers overall more comprehensive coverage.

Winner: The Platinum Card® from American Express

When To Use Amex Gold vs. Platinum

The Amex Gold is best utilized when making purchases at restaurants worldwide, as it earns 4 Membership Rewards points per dollar spent (up to a maximum of $50,000 in spend starting on January 1, 2025), which shouldn’t be a concern unless you usually spend around $1,000 a week in restaurants. If you also regularly spend on takeout and delivery services from U.S. restaurants or U.S. supermarkets, you should use the Gold Amex as those purchases also earn 4X per dollar spent. Earning 4X at U.S. supermarkets is capped at $25,000 a year.

The Platinum Amex instead, does not bonus any purchases on dining or supermarkets, so it should remain in your wallet when visiting those places. Instead, use it for purchasing flights directly from airlines as it will help you earn 5 Membership Rewards points per dollar spent, up to $500,000 spent on that category every calendar year, or prepaid hotel reservations booked through Amex Travel.

Avoiding an Annual Fee

The Amex Platinum and the Amex Gold Cards charge annual fees that will cost you several hundred dollars annually. If you can’t stomach the thought of shelling out that much for a card, even with all the perks and extras, consider a card like the Amex EveryDay® Credit Card*, which carries no annual fee and earns 2 Membership Rewards points per dollar at US supermarkets on up to $6,000 per year in purchases (then 1X), 2 points per dollar when using the card to book trips through American Express Travel and 1 point for every eligible dollar on everything else. Plus, use the card 20 or more times on purchases in a billing period and earn 20% extra points on those purchases.

Skipping the Perks

If you aren’t much of a traveler, the high-end perks with a premium travel card like the Amex Platinum likely won’t make that high annual fee worth it. Similarly, the Gold Card’s dining and Uber credits may not be helpful if you earn 4 points per dollar by buying groceries to cook at home.

Earning a Simple, Flat Rate on All of Your Spending

Using the Amex Platinum and the Amex Gold requires more mental energy than a card that earns the same flat rate on every purchase. If you feel like it’s more hassle than it’s worth to remember which card to use when, consider a card like the Citi Double Cash® Card, which has no annual fee and earns 2% cash back on all purchases—1% when purchases are made and another 1% when they’re paid off.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Is Amex Gold easier to get than Platinum?

Both the Amex Gold and The Platinum Card® from American Express are premium products offered to consumers with good to excellent credit.

Is Amex Gold or Amex Platinum better?

The Amex Gold and Amex Platinum are different products, meant for different consumers. If you travel frequently and want access to airline lounges, luxury travel experiences, hotel status and travel insurance protections, go for The Platinum. If you spend more at grocery stores or restaurants, the Gold will be more rewarding.

Is it worth upgrading Amex Gold to Platinum?

American Express periodically offers lucrative upgrade offers to Gold cardholders who want to step up to The Platinum Card® from American Express. Before upgrading, be sure to understand how what you earn on your spending will change and that your annual fee will increase significantly.

How many American Express cards can I have?

Many people find that having more than one American Express card can give them additional opportunities to earn rewards or enjoy the benefits of different card products. It’s been circulated among points and miles maximizers that four or five Amex Cards are the maximum you can hold at any one time and applying for any more above that will result in a denial of your application.
