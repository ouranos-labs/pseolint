Burial insurance can provide a financial safety net for your loved ones when you pass away. It can help cover funeral costs, medical bills and other final expenses. But unlike traditional life insurance policies, it provides smaller coverage amounts and won’t necessarily act as income replacement in your absence or pay for larger debts.

With the median cost of a funeral, burial and casket at $8,300, according to the National Funeral Directors Association, choosing the right policy is crucial. However, selecting an insurer can be challenging due to the many options available. We’ve reviewed top insurers, considering factors such as health status, waiting periods and coverage options to guide you in finding the best burial insurance company.

Methodology

To choose our winners for the best burial insurance, we started by analyzing 14 policies from 12 companies. We then narrowed down our list to the top 10 policies from eight companies. From there, we judged each company across seven categories essential to good policies. We analyzed more than 300 data points and used more than a dozen primary data sources during our research. We chose winners based on their overall scores and our editorial judgment. Read our full methodology here.

Mutual of Omaha’s Guaranteed Whole Life Insurance emerges as our best overall choice for burial insurance. Who needs burial insurance?

  • You have health concerns: Individuals with health issues who might not qualify for other types of life insurance can find the no-medical-exam requirement of burial insurance appealing.
  • You don’t want to leave a financial burden: Those looking to help cover funeral expenses, outstanding debts, medical bills and other end-of-life costs to ease their loved ones’ financial load after they pass away may find burial insurance helpful.
  • You’re on a tight budget: People on a set monthly income may find it easier to afford a burial insurance policy since the costs stay the same and don’t increase over time.

What is burial insurance?

Burial insurance is a type of whole life insurance policy that typically offers a smaller death benefit than most other types of life insurance. It’s intended to cover funeral expenses when a loved one passes away. It doesn’t usually require a medical exam, which makes it easier for many people to qualify for coverage.

Often referred to as funeral insurance or final expense insurance, burial insurance guarantees a lump-sum payment that helps family members cover costs like funeral services, burial or cremation and any remaining medical bills or debts after you pass away.

Burial insurance is a permanent policy, meaning it won’t expire as long as premium payments are current. It also accrues cash value you can borrow against for financial needs while you’re still alive.

Generally, coverage amounts for burial insurance range from $2,000 to $50,000, depending on the insurer and policy you choose.

Types of burial insurance

Burial insurance has two main types: simplified issue and guaranteed issue. Each one has a different application process and offers different coverage options.

Simplified issue burial insurance

Simplified issue burial insurance is an excellent option for healthier individuals. Applicants need to answer a few health-related questions but are not subjected to a medical examination. This type of policy is characterized by its relatively quick approval process and offers coverage amounts that can reach $40,000 or $50,000.

Guaranteed issue burial insurance

Guaranteed issue burial insurance provides a valuable solution for people whose age or health status may prevent them from securing a traditional life insurance policy — or even a simplified issue policy. Since it doesn’t require any health screenings or questions, virtually anyone who meets the age criteria can qualify. As a result, most applications are simple and approved quickly.

However, compared to simplified issue policies, the coverage amounts for guaranteed issue policies are generally capped at $25,000 and the premiums are usually higher.

Different factors can directly contribute to how much you’ll pay for a burial insurance policy, such as:

  • Age: Age is typically the most significant factor that affects costs. The older you are at the time of purchase, the higher the premiums will be.
  • Gender: Gender also plays a role, with males typically paying more than females.
  • Coverage amount: Monthly premiums will also vary depending on the desired coverage amount. A policy with a higher payout will lead to higher monthly rates.
  • Health status: Health can be a factor affecting burial insurance costs, with some insurers requiring health assessments to help determine rates and coverage options.

Pros and cons of burial insurance

ProsCons
  • Covers end-of-life expenses
  • No medical exam required for most policies
  • Permanent coverage
  • Guaranteed acceptance for most applicants
  • Higher premiums than traditional life insurance policies relative to your coverage amount
  • Lower payouts than traditional life insurance policies
  • May have a waiting period
  • Less value when comparing premiums to coverage

Pros explained

  • Covers end-of-life expenses: Burial insurance specifically helps handle the costs associated with funerals, burials, cremation and any outstanding medical bills to reduce the financial strain on loved ones.
  • No medical exam required for most policies: This makes it easier for many people to qualify for coverage.
  • Permanent coverage: Once approved, your coverage won’t expire, assuming premiums are paid each month.
  • Guaranteed acceptance for most applicants: Most people can obtain coverage, even those previously denied by other insurers due to health concerns.

Cons explained

  • Higher premiums than traditional life insurance policies relative to your coverage amount: Since burial insurance doesn’t usually require a medical exam, it typically has higher premiums than a traditional term life or whole life policy. This is because insurers aren’t able to properly assess their risk to insure you.
  • Lower payouts than traditional life insurance policies: The death benefits of burial insurance are usually lower when compared to other types of life insurance, which might not fully cover all final expenses. This may not be the best route if you’re looking to cover a larger debt or provide income replacement for your loved ones.
  • May have a waiting period: Burial insurance policies may have a waiting period of two or three years before it’ll pay out the full coverage amount. If you pass away during this time frame, then your policy will either return your premium payments with interest back to your beneficiaries or pay out a reduced death benefit, depending on your insurer’s conditions. In most cases, this doesn’t apply to accidental deaths.
  • Less value when comparing premiums to coverage: Although premiums are generally affordable, the value of the coverage you’re receiving may be lower than opting for another type of life insurance policy with a greater coverage amount. Additionally, if you have an accelerated death benefit rider for terminal or chronic illnesses and use part of your coverage to help pay medical bills while you’re still alive, your beneficiaries may not have enough funds to pay for funeral-related costs.

How to get the best burial insurance

Finding the best burial insurance means carefully considering various factors that meet your needs and budget, such as:

  • Assess your coverage needs: Determine if your primary goal is to cover funeral and burial costs or if you also wish to leave funds for your loved ones to handle other end-of-life expenses like remaining medical bills or debts. Also consider if you’d want to use some of your life insurance coverage after being diagnosed with a terminal or chronic condition. This will help you decide how much coverage to purchase.
  • Evaluate your budget: Determine how much you can afford to pay in premiums each month. Burial insurance offers different coverage options at costs that won’t strain your finances.
  • Consider your health situation: If you have health issues, choose a guaranteed issue burial insurance policy that doesn’t require a health questionnaire as part of the application. On the flip side, if you’re relatively healthy, opting for a simplified issue policy can help you save on premiums.
  • Compare quotes and insurance companies: Research and compare different policies and insurers to find the best rates and coverage options that match your specific needs.

Is burial insurance worth it?

Since burial insurance is typically a type of no-exam life insurance, it can be a beneficial option if you can’t qualify for a traditional life policy because of age or health issues. Burial insurance can also be worth it if your family will need help paying for your funeral and other end-of-life expenses.

However, if you have enough funds in your savings or retirement accounts or through other methods like another life insurance policy, then you may be able to forgo purchasing burial insurance.

Additionally, if you’re generally healthy and willing to undergo a medical exam, then you can often find better value and higher coverage amounts with different types of life insurance like term life or whole life. This may be the best life insurance option for families with young children or with substantial debt like a mortgage. You may pay more in premiums each month, but your beneficiaries can receive a greater payout, potentially making it more worthwhile in the long run.

Alternatives to burial insurance

Buying final expense life insurance has its perks, but if you’re looking for other options, here are a few alternatives to consider:

Term life insurance

Term life insurance is an affordable way to get significant coverage for a set period, such as 10 to 30 years. It’s ideal for those looking to protect their family’s financial future without a high cost. However, renewing the policy or purchasing a new one will likely be more expensive once the term ends. This type of insurance can also provide larger amounts of money, which can help with things other than funeral costs, like replacing lost income or paying off large debts.

Whole life insurance

Whole life insurance offers coverage that lasts your entire life if you keep paying the premiums. It also includes a cash value feature where some of your money can grow over time, which you can borrow from if needed. These policies can pay more money when you die than burial insurance, but they are more expensive.

Preneed insurance or contract

Preneed insurance or prepaid funeral contracts are options where you make a deal directly with a funeral service provider. First, you select the funeral services and products you want. Then, you either take out a burial policy that pays the funds to the funeral provider instead of your family or you pay the provider directly with savings, a trust or an investment vehicle.

Methodology

To find the best burial insurance companies, we started by examining 14 policies from 12 companies and then narrowed it down to the top-rated 10 policies from eight companies.

For each company, we analyzed seven crucial categories, leveraging financial strength ratings from credit agency AM Best, complaint data from the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) and various policy details advertised by the insurers.

Overall, we worked with over 300 data points to identify winning companies. The following sections describe the category we scored and the weight we gave that category in our scoring model.

Minimum and maximum death benefit options (20%)

Most insurers set a minimum death benefit (or face value) you are required to purchase. They also won’t typically provide coverage over a specific threshold. Policies that had a wider range of death benefit options between the minimum and maximum death benefit amounts received higher scores since this indicates greater flexibility when it comes to choosing a coverage amount appropriate for your needs.

Minimum and maximum issue age (20%)

You usually can’t apply for life insurance if you’re younger or older than the insurance company’s age limits. Policies that had a lower minimum issue age of 45 and a higher maximum issue age of 85 received higher scores.

Medical exam and health questionnaire requirements (20%)

Part of the hassle of applying for traditional life insurance involves taking a medical exam so underwriters can properly assess your health and risk and assign a rate accordingly. Burial insurance is typically a no-exam option, but some insurers require you to complete a health questionnaire. Policies that don’t require medical exams or health questionnaires received higher scores.

Waiting period (20%)

Many burial insurance policies have a waiting period of two or three years before they’ll pay out your full death benefit to your beneficiaries. This means that if you pass away during this period (generally due to any cause other than an accident), your beneficiary may receive a reduced or graded death benefit or a return of all premiums paid into the policy plus interest. Policies with no waiting period received the highest scores, followed by those that only impose a waiting period based on your health status. Lastly, policies with shorter waiting periods received better scores than those with longer waiting periods.

State availability (10%)

Not all insurance companies operate in all states. Some policyholders prefer working with national insurers, while others prefer working with ones that only work in their regions. Having access to adequate coverage is an important first step when analyzing insurance companies. For this category, we researched how many states each insurer operates in. Companies that offered policies in fewer than 25 states or have specific membership requirements received a scoring penalty.

Financial stability (5%)

AM Best is a well-respected independent credit rating agency that evaluates insurance companies’ financial strength. By analyzing insurers’ balance sheets, risk management, operating performance and other factors, AM Best assigns a financial strength rating (FSR) based on the insurers’ abilities to pay out on claims and cover other obligations.

Complaint rate (5%)

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) calculates an index for insurance companies based on the company’s share of premiums and complaints filed with the NAIC. An index of 1.00 indicates the insurer received the expected number of complaints for a company of its size. An index below 1.00 means the company received fewer complaints than expected for a company of its size and vice versa. We took the average index for each company from the past three years.

What didn’t make the cut

Of the 10 burial insurance policies we analyzed for our final rankings, four did not win a category: Corebridge Financial’s SimpliNow Legacy, AARP’s Guaranteed Acceptance Life Insurance from New York Life, State Farm’s Guaranteed Issue Final Expense Insurance and American Family’s DreamSecure Senior Whole Life Insurance. All four policies had overall scores below our 3-star threshold based on our methodology.

Frequently asked questions (FAQs)

Getting burial insurance is simple since there are no medical exams involved, although you may be required to answer health questions. If you’re between 50 and 80, you can typically be approved for a policy within a few days. Some insurers will insure people as young as 45 and as old as 85.

Yes, the money from burial insurance can be used for more than just burial expenses. Your family can freely use the death benefit for a variety of needs, including medical expenses, credit card debt, day-to-day expenses, legal costs or any other purpose they decide.

To start a burial insurance claim, the beneficiary must contact the insurance provider immediately after the insured person passes away. They will likely need to show some form of identification, fill out a claim form and provide an official copy of the death certificate.

The cost of burial insurance depends on factors like age, health, gender and how much coverage you want. People who are older, those with health issues and men often pay more. In addition, the more coverage you choose, the higher your monthly payments will be.

Whether burial insurance includes a waiting period depends on the insurance company and the policy you choose. In some cases, policies may have a waiting period where beneficiaries receive a reduced (or no) death benefit or a return of the premiums paid into the policy plus interest if you pass away during the designated time frame. Conversely, some policies have no waiting period, so the full death benefit is available from day one of the policy.
