By

There are a lot of advantages to using a credit union vs. a bank. One of the most significant credit union benefits is their prioritization of member satisfaction over shareholder profit. That means they often offer higher deposit rates, lower borrowing costs and fewer fees.

Why get a credit union account?

  • Member-owned: The people who use the products and services at a credit union (members) own the institution, so they have a say in how it operates.
  • Not-for-profit: Unlike banks that hand profits over to shareholders, credit unions return profits to their members through better rates and fewer fees.
  • Safety: The National Credit Union Association (NCUA) protects deposits up to $250,000 per person at federally insured credit unions, making them just as safe as banks.

Credit unions vs. banks

Although they often provide similar products, there are significant differences between banks vs. credit unions.

While banks are for-profit financial institutions owned by shareholders, credit unions are not-for-profit and owned by their members. Because they are not-for-profit, they may offer better rates and lower fees than banks.

Unlike banks, credit unions generally require you to become a member before you can bank with them. Although it can be easy to qualify for membership at some credit unions, others may only serve people who meet specific eligibility requirements, such as living or working in the area.

Although credit unions typically provide a variety of financial services, they may not offer as many as banks. They also tend to have fewer branch locations, ATMs and digital banking tools.

The advantages of a credit union

The primary goal of a bank is turning a profit for its shareholders. Banks often charge customers higher fees and have higher deposit and balance requirements to maximize profits.

However, at a credit union, the members who use the products and services are the shareholders. As a member, you’re a partial owner of the credit union, meaning you can vote on new policies and elect the board of directors that manage it.

Since credit unions are not-for-profit, they typically try to pass on any profits they earn to their members through reduced fees, higher deposit rates and lower lending rates. It creates a symbiotic relationship between members because one person’s deposits can become a loan for someone else.

Credit union eligibility

Credit unions typically require membership, meaning you’ll have to meet certain requirements before using their products and services.

Eligibility criteria vary, but they’re often based on your location, employment, associations or other aspects. For example, credit unions for military members may require you or a relative to be tied to the armed forces, Department of Defense or National Guard.

Some qualification requirements may prevent you from becoming a member; however, many credit unions offer an easy way to become eligible. You may be able to pay a membership fee or donate a small amount to charity to join.

Financial products offered by credit unions

While credit unions may not provide as wide an array as banks, they can still offer a variety of financial products and services. Here are some of the financial products you could find at a credit union:

  • Savings accounts
  • Checking accounts
  • Money market accounts
  • Share certificates*
  • Credit cards
  • Mortgages
  • Home equity loans
  • Home equity lines of credit (HELOCs)
  • Auto loans
  • Student loans
  • Personal loans
  • Individual retirement accounts (IRAs)

*Share certificates are similar to certificates of deposit (CDs) found at a bank.

Safety of credit union deposits

Deposits held at a federally insured credit union are just as secure as they would be at a bank.

Like the protection the Federal Deposit Insurance Corp. (FDIC) provides for bank deposits, the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) safeguards deposits at credit unions that are federally insured.

The NCUA’s coverage limit is $250,000 per depositor, which is the same level of insurance you would receive through an FDIC-insured bank. This insurance guarantees that if the credit union ever experiences a bank run or failure, your money is safe up to the NCUA limit.

According to the NCUA, no depositor has ever lost a penny of their insured savings at a federally insured credit union.

Questions to ask when selecting a credit union

If you qualify for membership at multiple credit unions, there are a few questions to ask yourself so you can pick the right one:

  • Does the credit union provide all the financial products and services I need?
  • What APYs does the credit union offer on its deposit accounts?
  • What are the APRs on loans through the credit union?
  • How many branch locations and ATMs does the institution have?
  • What are other customers saying about the credit unions online?

How to join a credit union

Once you find the credit union you want to join, you’ll need to apply for membership. You may have the option of applying online, by mail or in person.

The credit union will likely ask you for basic information to complete your application. Usually, you’ll need to provide:

  • A valid government-issued ID, such as a driver’s license or passport.
  • Your date of birth.
  • Your Social Security number or taxpayer identification number.
  • Proof of your address, such as a utility bill or insurance card.
  • Your phone number and email address.

Once approved, you can choose the type of account you want (savings, checking, joint account, etc.) and make your first deposit. You may be able to fund the account with an electronic transfer, cash, a check, debit card or money order.

Methodology

CNN Underscored Money analyzed more than 15 financial institutions to develop the rankings for the best credit unions. We ranked each credit union on 19 data points across seven categories.

Here are the categories we analyzed and how we weighted each:

Fees (20%)

Fees are a critical factor to consider because they can eat away at any interest earnings, especially if an account has a high monthly maintenance fee.

APY (20%)

APY is a critical factor for credit unions because this determines how quickly your deposit will grow. Accounts with higher APYs scored better.

Product offerings (20%)

Many customers deciding on a credit union will want an institution that offers a full range of financial products.

Customer experience (10%)

It’s important to have a credit union that’s trustworthy and easy to reach when you need help. We considered customer service availability and Trustpilot ratings in this category.

Digital experience (10%)

You’ll want a credit union with a usable app and plenty of online features so that it’s easy to bank from anywhere. We analyzed app reviews and the ability to bank online.

Minimums (10%)

Deposit and balance requirements can be an obstacle for some customers when it comes to opening an account. Credit unions with low or no minimums scored higher.

Access (10%)

Many customers want to be able to make transactions at branches or ATMs easily, so credit unions with a wider network of locations scored higher.

Frequently asked questions (FAQs)

The requirements to become a member vary between credit unions. Some may require you to live or work locally, while others simply require a small donation to charity.

Credit unions pass on profits to their members, so they may offer lower fees than banks.

While credit unions may offer mobile banking, the apps may not be as comprehensive as those of online or traditional banks.

Credit unions often donate to local charities and provide financial education to their members. Since all members own a credit union, one person’s savings becomes a loan for someone else, which can support small businesses and the community.
