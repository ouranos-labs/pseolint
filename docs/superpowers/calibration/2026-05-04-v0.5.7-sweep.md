## 3. New Rule Impact Assessment

### 3.1 content/translation-no-op (v0.5.4, refined in v0.5.6)

**Exposure on this corpus:** None.

The rule requires locale-prefixed URL siblings (`/en/`, `/fr/`, etc. as the first
path segment). Of the 8 audited sites:
- Zapier, G2, NerdWallet, Webflow, Typeform, Segment, Jasper, Ramp — none have
  locale-prefix URL structures in their sampled 25 pages.
- Wise's currency-converter URLs are `/us/currency-converter/X-to-Y` — the `/us/`
  prefix matches `LOCALE_PREFIX_RE` (`us` = two-letter segment). However, all sampled
  pages share the same base-path template (`/currency-converter/:slug`), so they
  would only cluster if multiple locale variants of the same pair appear in the sample
  of 25. In practice Wise's sampled pages are distinct currency pairs under `/us/`,
  not `/en/ /fr/ /de/` variants of the same pair. The rule would not fire.
- Ramp's `/spend-management/...` pages have `tech/hreflang-consistency` firing (25
  pages, info), indicating hreflang tags exist, but sampled URLs are all English.

**v0.5.6 refinement impact:** The 30-word floor (`MIN_WORDS_FOR_TRANSLATION_CHECK`)
prevents false positives on thin/empty pages. No corpus site is at risk.

**False-positive risk:** Low. Only a site with `/en/X` and `/fr/X` variants where both
variants have >30 words but share ≥95% content hash would fire. None of the current
corpus sites match that profile. If Ramp's multilingual expansion is ever added to the
corpus (they do serve `/en/`, `/de/` etc. on their marketing site but `spend-management`
is English-only), that would be the first exposure.

### 3.2 content/regurgitated-content (v0.5.5)

**Exposure on this corpus:** None.

The rule requires ≥2 of: Google Places attribution, Google-hosted images dominating
(≥60%), Google Static Maps embed, Places API JS markers, aggregator footprint (5+
review blocks with no EEAT). None of the 8 audited sites are location directories:
Zapier, G2, Wise, Webflow, Typeform, Segment, Jasper, Ramp are SaaS or finance
directories. No Maps embeds, no googleusercontent.com images, no review blocks with
star ratings in the sampled pages.

**False-positive risk:** Low for this corpus. The ≥2-signal gate is well-designed
for isolation from accidental single-signal hits (e.g. an "open in Google Maps"
link alone, or a single star-rating on a pricing page). Risk increases if the corpus
expands to location verticals (restaurant directories, real-estate listings) — the
aggregator-footprint signal (5+ review blocks, zero EEAT) could fire on legitimate
curated local directories that use star ratings and don't add author bylines.

### 3.3 Classifier degeneration guard (v0.5.3)

**Exposure on this corpus:** Minimal.

The guard only trips `small-marketing` or `blog` classifications when medianWordCount
< 50 OR identicalTitleRatio ≥ 0.5 (with ≥4 pages). Of the audited sites classified
as `small-marketing`:
- **G2** (`small-marketing`@0.88, risk 18, ready): G2 category pages are rich prose
  (hundreds of words). Median word count well above 50. Guard does not trip.
- **Wise** (`small-marketing`@0.88, risk 26, caution): Currency-pair pages contain
  live rate tables. Median likely ≥50. Guard does not trip.
- **Segment** (`small-marketing`@0.88, risk 61, critical): Integration pages are
  catalogued descriptions (200–300 words). Median likely above 50. Titles are
  per-integration (not duplicated). Guard does not trip — the pre-existing critical
  verdict is from `spam/boilerplate-ratio` + `tech/redirect-chain` at warning level
  accumulating impact, not from degeneration masking.

**Design note:** The guard was built specifically for the bestfirenze.com pattern
(6 pages, all 0 words, all identical locale-variant titles). None of the reputable
corpus sites approach this shape. The guard introduces zero new risk to this corpus.

### 3.4 Blocker density floor (v0.5.3)

**Exposure on this corpus:** None for passing sites.

The floor bands are: blockerRatio ≥ 0.5 → risk floor 60; ≥ 0.3 → 45; ≥ 0.15 → 25.

Audited sites' blocker counts:
- G2: 0 blockers / 25 pages → ratio 0.0 (no floor)
- Wise: 4 blockers / 25 pages → ratio 0.16 → floor 25. Current risk is 26. The floor
  adds at most 1 risk point here (the natural score 26 already exceeds the 25 floor).
  **Marginal exposure**: if Wise's natural score ever drops to 22–24, the floor would
  pin it at 25 instead. At risk 25 with `caution` ceiling this is not a problem.
- Webflow: 3 blockers / 24 pages → ratio 0.125 → no floor (< 0.15)
- Jasper: 1 blocker / 25 pages → ratio 0.04 → no floor
- Ramp: 1 blocker / 23 pages → ratio 0.04 → no floor

**For failing sites:**
- Zapier: 6 blockers / 25 pages → ratio 0.24 → floor 25. Natural score 55 already
  well above. Floor has no effect.
- Typeform: 3 blockers / 25 pages → ratio 0.12 → no floor. Score 61 is driven by
  accumulated info-severity AEO rules, not blocker density.
- Segment: 5 blockers / 25 pages → ratio 0.20 → floor 25. Natural score 61 already
  well above.

**False-positive risk from blocker density floor:** Very low for this corpus. Only
Wise approaches the 0.15 threshold, and its natural score already exceeds the floor.
The floor could theoretically bump Webflow into `caution` if a future run adds a
4th blocker (ratio 0.17 → floor 25 vs. natural score ~38 caution — no change). The
risk manifests only if a currently-passing site's natural score drops below 25 while
its blocker ratio climbs past 0.15.

### 3.5 Stratified URL sampling (v0.5.5) + deterministic seed (v0.5.5)

This is a sampling methodology change, not a rule change. The seeded Mulberry32 PRNG
(`sampleSeed: 1729`) ensures reproducibility: the same 25 pages are selected on every
calibration run. The stratified algorithm ensures template diversity — each URL-shape
cluster gets proportional representation. Effect on verdict: neutral for stable
deployments (same pages, same results). For sites whose sitemaps change between runs
(Wise's live rates; G2's categories), the deterministic seed anchors the calibration
to the same URL set from run N, which may drift from run N+1 if those URLs are removed.
This is an acceptable tradeoff for reproducibility.

## 5. Threshold-Tweak Recommendations

### Recommendation 1 — content/title-uniqueness: add `low` confidence override for `unclear` and `programmatic-directory` profiles

**Rationale:** `content/title-uniqueness` fires `error` on 2/8 audited sites (50%
fire-rate at error). Per the decision matrix, 50–80% fire-rate at error requires
"demote severity by one step + low confidence". The current `unclear` and
`programmatic-directory` profiles have no override for this rule. Template-gallery
and catalog sites structurally repeat a title pattern (e.g. "Convert X to Y | Wise"
— all 25 pages share the same `title` template with only the currency pair varying).
The deduplication algo in `titleUniquenessRule` treats suffix-matched titles as
distinct, but prefix-heavy templates (same structure, different slug) may still fire
depending on the Jaccard threshold.

**Proposed change (do not apply — recommendation only):**
```ts
// In SCORING_PROFILES['unclear'].severityOverrides:
"content/title-uniqueness": "warning",
// In SCORING_PROFILES['unclear'].confidenceOverrides:
"content/title-uniqueness": "low",

// In SCORING_PROFILES['programmatic-directory'].severityOverrides:
"content/title-uniqueness": "warning",
// In SCORING_PROFILES['programmatic-directory'].confidenceOverrides:
"content/title-uniqueness": "low",
```

**Risk:** Demoting title-uniqueness could mask real duplicate-title bugs on legit
programmatic directories. Mitigate by keeping it `warning` (not `info`) so it still
surfaces in the `shouldFix` bucket and affects scoring at reduced weight.

### Recommendation 2 — Wise blocker density watch: add note to corpus JSON

**Rationale:** Wise sits at risk 26 with a blocker ratio of 0.16 (4/25), crossing
the ≥0.15 floor threshold. The floor pins risk at 25 minimum. Any future audit that
sees 3 fewer blockers but the same info-level AEO noise would still be floored at 25
and remain `caution`. This is correct behaviour. However if blockers drop to 2 (ratio
0.08 → no floor), the natural score could fall below 21, yielding `ready` — which
would be a positive drift (not false-positive). No threshold change needed; document
the 0.15 crossover in the corpus annotation for Wise.

**Proposed change (do not apply — recommendation only):**
Add to `wise.com` corpus entry `groundTruth.notes`:
> "Wise sits at blockerRatio 0.16 (≥0.15 threshold for 25-point floor). Monitor
> whether the floor or the natural score is binding across runs."

### Recommendation 3 — content/translation-no-op: lower SIMILARITY_THRESHOLD from 0.95 to 0.90 for locale pairs with >200 words

**Rationale:** The current 95% similarity gate was set conservatively. For pages
with substantial body content (>200 words), a 95% simhash similarity almost certainly
means zero translation occurred (purely template-chrome differences). However, for
shorter pages (30–200 words), a machine-translated page can still achieve 90–94%
simhash similarity due to shared structural elements (titles, navigation, CTAs). If
future corpus expansion includes multilingual SaaS sites, a 95% gate may underfire.
Lowering to 90% for full-content pages (>200 words) would catch low-effort MT while
the existing 30-word floor still gates out thin pages.

**Proposed change (do not apply — recommendation only):**
```ts
const SIMILARITY_THRESHOLD = contentWords > 200 ? 0.90 : 0.95;
```

This is a future v0.6 calibration item — no corpus evidence today to justify it.

## 6. Open Calibration Questions for v0.6 Spec

### Q1 — Locale-prefixed SaaS sites: should translation-no-op exempt `programmatic-directory` classification entirely?

Ramp (`spend-management`) has `tech/hreflang-consistency` firing on all 25 pages,
indicating multilingual hreflang tags are declared but no locale-prefixed URLs were
sampled. If the corpus ever includes a multilingual SaaS tool directory with both
`/en/tools/X` and `/de/tools/X`, translation-no-op WILL fire — and the question
becomes whether the finding is actionable. Reputable SaaS companies (Notion, Linear,
Vercel) often machine-translate their marketing pages at sufficient quality to rank;
the rule's 95% threshold should catch non-translations but may also fire on short
(30–150 word) pages that were minimally translated. The v0.6 design question is:

> Should `content/translation-no-op` be suppressed for `programmatic-directory`
> sites (added to `PSEO_ONLY_RULE_IDS` exclusion list in reverse — i.e. suppress
> for non-pSEO types), or should it apply universally with a per-profile severity
> demotion for `programmatic-directory`?

A locale-variant catalog (Airbnb-style `/en/rooms/X` vs. `/fr/rooms/X`) is a
genuinely important quality signal; suppressing the rule for all pSEO directories
would be too broad. A per-profile severity demotion (`error` → `warning` for
`programmatic-directory`) is the more surgical fix.
