The Internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks that uses the Internet protocol suite (TCP/IP) to communicate between networks and devices. It is a network of networks that consists of private, public, academic, business, and government networks of local to global scope, linked by a broad array of electronic, wireless, and optical networking technologies. The Internet carries a vast range of information resources and services, such as the interlinked hypertext documents and applications of the World Wide Web, electronic mail, telephony, and file sharing.

The origins of the Internet date back to research that enabled the time-sharing of computer resources and the development of packet switching in the 1960s. The funding of a new U.S. backbone by the National Science Foundation in the 1980s, as well as private funding for other commercial extensions, encouraged worldwide participation in the development of new networking technologies, and the merger of many networks. The linking of commercial networks and enterprises by the early 1990s marks the beginning of the transition to the modern Internet, and generated a sustained exponential growth as generations of institutional, personal, and mobile computers were connected to the network.

The Internet is used by billions of people worldwide. It has fundamentally changed how people communicate, work, learn, and entertain themselves. The Internet has enabled new forms of personal interactions through instant messaging, internet forums, and social networking. Online shopping has grown exponentially both for major retailers and small businesses and entrepreneurs. Supply chain management is changing rapidly as online collaboration is easier. The internet has completely transformed the world of entertainment.

The World Wide Web was developed by British computer scientist Tim Berners-Lee in 1989-91 while working at CERN, the European particle physics laboratory in Geneva, Switzerland. The Web is a system of interconnected documents and other resources linked by hyperlinks and URLs. The Web is accessed using a web browser, which retrieves web pages from web servers and displays them on the user's computer screen. The most popular web browsers include Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, and Microsoft Edge.

Internet access is available in a variety of forms, including dial-up, cable, digital subscriber line (DSL), and fiber optic connections. The fastest and most reliable form of Internet access is fiber optic, which uses light transmitted through glass or plastic fibers to carry data at very high speeds.

The Internet has raised many important issues, including privacy, security, and intellectual property. The use of the Internet for communication and information sharing has given rise to concerns about privacy and the collection of personal data by companies and governments. The Internet has also become a major target for cybercriminals, who use it to steal personal information, commit fraud, and launch cyberattacks.

The Internet has had a profound impact on the global economy. It has enabled new forms of commerce, including electronic commerce (e-commerce) and digital advertising. The Internet has also given rise to new industries, such as social media, cloud computing, and the sharing economy.
